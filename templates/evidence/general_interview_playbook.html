General interview playbook

Know what to do when the interview changes direction.

Read only what you need. Open a stage, then open the exact topic you want. The rest stays out of the way.

Back to Interview Hub

Quick framework

Ask a useful clarifying question

Restate → identify → ask → proceed
1

Restate

Briefly repeat your understanding of the task.

2

Identify

Name the ambiguity that could change your approach.

3

Ask

Ask one specific question instead of listing every uncertainty.

4

Proceed

If it stays open, state a reasonable assumption and continue.

Communication toolkitClarify, think aloud, recover and disagree professionally.5 topics
When to ask clarifying questionsAsk when missing information could materially change the answer.

Ask about

  • The expected outcome or definition of success.
  • Ambiguous inputs, users, scope or constraints.
  • Edge cases that produce meaningfully different behaviour.
  • Permitted tools or assumptions in a timed assessment.

Useful wording

  • “My understanding is that we need to return X. Should Y also be included?”
  • “Does the input guarantee X, or should I handle Y as well?”
  • “If there is no preference, I will assume X and explain the trade-off.”

Avoid

  • Asking what the prompt already states.
  • Listing ten questions before showing any understanding.
  • Continuing silently when one ambiguity could invalidate the solution.
How to think aloud without ramblingExpose decisions, not every passing thought.

What to do

  • State the goal, current hypothesis and next check.
  • Explain why you choose one option over another.
  • Separate facts from assumptions.
  • Pause while reading; silence for a few seconds is normal.
  • Summarise after a long investigation.

Useful wording

  • “The important constraint is X, so I am choosing Y.”
  • “I have two possible causes. I will test the cheaper one first.”
  • “Let me summarise where I am before I continue.”
What to do when you get stuckShrink the problem and make the next useful move visible.

What to do

  • Restate what is known and unknown.
  • Return to the smallest failing example.
  • Check assumptions and intermediate values before redesigning.
  • Ask for a directional hint only after showing what you tried.
  • Use the hint, then explain the updated reasoning yourself.

Useful wording

  • “I am stuck between X and Y. I ruled out Z because…”
  • “I am going to reduce this to the smallest example and trace it.”
  • “Could you confirm whether I am looking in the right area?”
How to handle an unknown answerBe honest, connect adjacent knowledge and explain how you would verify it.

What to do

  • Name the exact part you do not know.
  • Connect the nearest relevant experience without pretending it is identical.
  • Explain how you would verify the answer.
  • Do not invent library behaviour, syntax or production facts.

Useful wording

  • “I have not used that service directly, so I do not want to guess.”
  • “The closest experience I have is X, where the principle was Y.”
  • “I would verify that in the official documentation and a small test.”
How to challenge an assumption politelyGround the concern in the shared goal and trade-off.

What to do

  • Confirm the shared goal first.
  • Explain the risk rather than calling the idea wrong.
  • Offer an alternative and invite correction.
  • Change course when a missing constraint is supplied.

Useful wording

  • “That could work. My concern is X because it may affect Y.”
  • “Would it be reasonable to compare that with Z before deciding?”
  • “I may be missing a constraint, but with the current information I would prefer X.”
Before the interviewPrepare evidence, role context and logistics.3 topics
Prepare your evidence bankChoose reusable examples instead of memorising dozens of answers.
  • Prepare a concise introduction and two or three project explanations.
  • Prepare STAR stories for ownership, conflict, failure, learning and collaboration.
  • Attach outcomes, decisions and lessons to every example.
  • Know which examples match the role's main requirements.
Research with a purposeResearch only what improves your answers or questions.
  • Understand the product, customers, role and likely team problems.
  • Know why the role fits your direction.
  • Prepare two questions the advert cannot answer.
  • Confirm the format, duration, platform and expected tools.
Set up the environmentRemove preventable technical and logistical failures.
  • Test the meeting link, microphone, camera, editor and connection.
  • Keep the job description, CV and blank notes available.
  • Join early enough to solve issues without arriving rushed.
  • For assessments, confirm repository setup and the test command.
Recruiter screenShow motivation, baseline fit and practical alignment.3 topics
Introduce yourselfConnect your background, strengths and reason for this role.
  • Start with your current professional identity.
  • Select two or three experiences relevant to the role.
  • End with why this is the logical next step.
  • Keep the first version around one minute.
Clarify the opportunityUnderstand the process and whether the role matches.
  • Ask what the new hire should contribute first.
  • Confirm interview stages and assessment format.
  • Ask how junior developers are supported and evaluated.
  • Clarify location, work pattern and timing where relevant.
Discuss gaps without disqualifying yourselfSeparate missing exposure from missing ability.
  • Acknowledge the exact gap.
  • Connect adjacent experience and your learning process.
  • Give evidence that you have learned unfamiliar tools before.
  • Do not claim experience you cannot support.
Behavioural interviewUse specific evidence to show how you act, decide and learn.3 topics
Build a strong STAR answerKeep context short and spend most of the answer on your actions.
  • Situation: only the context needed.
  • Task: your responsibility and success condition.
  • Action: what you personally did, why and in what order.
  • Result: outcome and evidence.
  • Reflection: what you learned or would improve.
Handle follow-up questionsExpect ownership, alternatives and credibility to be tested.
  • Explain why you chose that action.
  • Name alternatives and trade-offs.
  • State what you would do differently now.
  • Explain how teammates or users responded.
  • Keep details consistent with the original story.
Answer failure and conflict questionsShow ownership and learning rather than perfection.
  • Choose a real example with a meaningful but recoverable consequence.
  • Own your part without blaming others.
  • Explain the corrective and prevention steps.
  • For conflict, return the discussion to the shared goal.
Technical discussionDemonstrate fundamentals, structure and trade-offs.3 topics
Structure a technical answerDefine, explain, apply and qualify.
  • Start with a plain-language definition.
  • Explain the mechanism or components.
  • Connect it to a project or practical example.
  • Name a limitation or trade-off.
  • Check whether the interviewer wants deeper detail.
Approach a design questionClarify the goal before naming technologies.
  • Clarify users, scale, reliability, latency, security and operations.
  • Describe the simplest end-to-end flow first.
  • Identify data ownership and system boundaries.
  • Discuss one bottleneck at a time.
  • State what you would measure before optimising.
Explain project architectureTell the story of a request rather than listing the stack.
  • Start with the problem and users.
  • Trace one request through the system.
  • Explain the data model and external dependencies.
  • Highlight decisions you personally made.
  • Finish with testing, trade-offs and scaling.
Coding or repository assessmentMake understanding, implementation and verification visible.3 topics
Before writing codeConfirm behaviour and establish a baseline.
  • Read the whole task and restate the output.
  • Clarify constraints that change the approach.
  • Run supplied tests before editing an unfamiliar repository.
  • Trace a small example manually.
  • State the approach and complexity.
While implementingWork in small, explainable steps.
  • Reuse existing patterns and helpers.
  • Keep changes narrow and run focused checks early.
  • Name assumptions and edge cases.
  • Verify every AI suggestion.
  • Avoid unrelated refactoring during a timed task.
Before submittingProve more than “the visible test passed”.
  • Run targeted tests and the full suite.
  • Check boundaries, invalid input, permissions and errors.
  • Review the final diff.
  • Confirm you can explain every changed line.
  • State remaining limitations honestly.
Hiring-manager or final interviewConnect your evidence to the team's needs.2 topics
Show role-level judgementExplain priorities and decisions, not only tasks.
  • Explain how you balance speed, correctness and scope.
  • Give examples of asking for help at the right time.
  • Show how you communicate risk and progress.
  • Connect growth areas to real team work.
Evaluate the environmentUnderstand expectations, support and ownership.
  • Ask what success looks like in the first three months.
  • Ask how work is reviewed and feedback is given.
  • Ask how junior engineers gain context and ownership.
  • Ask what currently makes the team's work difficult.
Closing and follow-upEnd clearly and improve the next interview.3 topics
Questions to ask the interviewerAsk questions that affect your understanding or decision.
  • “What would you want the person in this role to improve first?”
  • “What distinguishes someone who does well on this team?”
  • “How does the team balance delivery with technical improvement?”
  • “What challenge is the team actively working through?”
Close the conversationReinforce interest without another long speech.
  • Thank the interviewer.
  • Briefly connect one role detail to your interest or experience.
  • Ask about the next step if it was not explained.
  • Do not introduce a completely new life story at the end.
Reflect afterwardsCapture evidence while details are fresh.
  • Record the questions and unclear answers.
  • Separate technical gaps from communication gaps.
  • Write improved answer points, not a script.
  • Record follow-ups and promised information.
  • Turn repeated gaps into future study or mock tasks.
